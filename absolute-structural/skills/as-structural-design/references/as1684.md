# House framing - AS 1684.2 (non-cyclonic, wind classes N1 to N4)

**Status marks.** `[C]` = matched to a published secondary source on 2026-11-16. `[U]` = not
matched - confirm against the standard in the Knowledge Vault.

**Every clause number, table number and value in this file is `[U]` unless marked `[C]`.**

AS 1684.2 is a deemed-to-satisfy standard: the answer comes from its span tables and
detailing rules, not from a capacity calculation. **The span tables are not reproduced in
this skill** - they run to thousands of entries across the supplements, and a remembered
span is not something to put on a drawing. This file covers how to set up the lookup, the
bracing and tie-down procedures, and what to record. Read the member size from Scott's copy
in the vault, or from a current manufacturer span table for the product.

Edition: AS 1684.2:2021, referenced by NCC 2022 `[C]`; Amendment 1:2024 is current and is
picked up by NCC 2025 `[C]`. State which one the schedule is based on. Many supplier guides
were written against the 2010 edition - table letters and numbers can differ.

Related parts: AS 1684.3 cyclonic areas (C1-C3), AS 1684.4 simplified (N1/N2, limited
geometry). Wind classification comes from AS 4055 (`loads.md`).

## 1. Is the job inside AS 1684.2? - Cl 1.4

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Building class | Class 1 and Class 10 |
| Wind classification | N1 to N4 `[C]` |
| Storeys of timber framing | up to two `[C]` |
| Building width | up to 16.0 m `[C]`, excluding eaves |
| Roof pitch | up to 35 degrees `[C]` |
| Wall height | up to 3000 mm `[C]` floor to ceiling (taller at gable ends) |
| Plan | rectangular, or a combination of rectangles (L, T, U) |
| Spacing of bracing walls | within Cl 1.4.8 / 8.3.6.7 |

AS 1684.4 (simplified) is tighter: 12 m width, 30 degree pitch, 2.7 m walls `[C]`.

If any limit is exceeded, that part needs first-principles design (`timber.md`) or an
engineer; say which limit and which members are affected.

## 2. Loads the tables assume

| Load | Value |
|---|---|
| Floor imposed | 1.5 kPa (and 1.8 kN point load) |
| Decks and balconies 1 m or more above ground | 2.0 kPa |
| Sheet roof | 10 kg/m2 class `[C]` (20 kg/m2 heavier sheet); with ceiling 40 kg/m2 |
| Tile roof | 60 kg/m2 class `[C]`; with ceiling 90 kg/m2 |
| Floor mass | 40 kg/m2 class for ordinary timber floors |

A floor with a tiled wet area on a thick bed, stone benchtops along a wall, a water bed, a
spa, or roof-mounted plant carries more than the tables assume. Check that member from
first principles.

## 3. Lookup inputs - Section 2.6

The tables are entered with a load width (how much floor or roof the member collects) and the
member's own span and spacing.

| Input | Meaning | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|
| FLW floor load width | width of floor carried by a bearer or wall | half the joist span each side for simple spans, plus any cantilever |
| RLW roof load width | width of roof carried by a wall, beam, underpurlin or lintel | along the rafter slope: half the rafter span each side supported, plus the eaves overhang |
| CLW ceiling load width | width of ceiling carried by a hanging beam or counter beam | half the ceiling joist span each side, horizontal |
| Roof area supported | plan area carried by a strutting beam, post or girder | from the strut or support layout |
| ULW uplift load width | width of roof a tie-down holds | Fig 9.5 |

Continuous joists or rafters put more than half the span onto the internal support - use
the figure for that arrangement. `python scripts/as_calc.py as1684-widths` does the simple
cases and shows the working.

Trussed roofs: RLW is taken from the truss span (half the span plus overhang, on the slope),
and walls under girder trusses take a concentrated load that the common-stud tables don't
cover - use the jamb stud / concentrated load tables and check the lintel.

## 4. Choosing the supplement and table

Each supplement is one wind-class group (N1/N2, N3 or N4) and one stress grade. Numbering
within a group, as far as could be confirmed `[C]`:

| Supp | Grade | Supp | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Seasoned softwood F5 | 8 | Seasoned hardwood F17 |
| 2 | Seasoned softwood F7 | 9 | Seasoned hardwood F27 |
| 3 | Seasoned softwood F8 | 10 | Unseasoned softwood F5 |
| 4 | MGP10 | 11 | Unseasoned softwood F7 |
| 5 | MGP12 | 12 | Unseasoned hardwood F8 |
| 6 | MGP15 | 13-15 | Unseasoned hardwood F11 and above (numbers not confirmed) |

Tables inside a supplement `[C]`: bearers 1-5; floor joists 6; wall studs 7-13; lower-storey
lintels 47-48; verandah, deck and post tables 49-53 (deck joists 50, verandah beams 51-52,
posts 53). Ceiling, roof and upper-storey lintel table numbers were not confirmed - read
them from the supplement. Quote supplement and table in the schedule ("N1/N2 Supp 4,
Table 6") so a certifier can find the entry.

Order of work, top down, because each level loads the next:
roof battens -> rafters or trusses -> underpurlins, struts, strutting beams, ridge beams ->
ceiling joists, hanging beams -> top plates -> studs, jamb studs, lintels -> bottom plates ->
floor joists -> bearers -> posts/stumps -> footings.

Engineered products (LVL, I-joists, glulam, floor trusses) are not in the supplements: use
the manufacturer's current span tables, which are written in the same FLW/RLW terms.

## 5. Bracing - Section 8

Procedure:
1. Wind classification (AS 4055).
2. Area of elevation for each wind direction: the projected area of the building above
   mid-height of the walls of the storey being braced (Fig 8.2), in m2.
3. Racking force = area x lateral wind pressure from Tables 8.1 to 8.5 (pressure depends on
   wind class, roof pitch, building width and whether the wind is on the side or the end).
4. Provide bracing walls whose total capacity is at least the racking force, in each
   direction, for each storey.
5. Check distribution and spacing.
6. Fix each bracing wall to the ceiling/roof diaphragm at the top and to the floor at the
   bottom (Tables 8.22-8.24).
7. Brace the subfloor where the floor sits on stumps, piers or posts (Cl 8.3.5).

Structural bracing capacities, Table 8.18, kN per metre of wall:

| Type | Description | kN/m |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Two diagonally opposed timber or metal angle braces | 0.8 `[C]` |
| (b) | Tensioned metal straps (crossed pairs) | 1.5 `[C]` |
| (c) | Timber and metal angle braces with fixed ends | 1.5 |
| (d) | Tensioned metal straps with stud straps (a plain 30 x 0.8 strap does not qualify) | 3.0 `[C]` |
| (e) | Diagonal timber wall lining or cladding | 2.1 |
| (g) | Structural plywood, standard nailing | 3.4 `[C]` (letter not confirmed) |
| (h) | Structural plywood, method A (M12 rods each end) / method B (no rods) | 6.4 `[C]` / 6.0 |

The 2021 table has more types than listed here (thin plywood, decorative plywood, hardboard
types A to E). Tabulated capacities assume JD4 framing; reduce by 15% for JD5 (MGP10) `[C]`
- the calculator's `--jd5` flag does this. 3.4 kN/m is the most a bracing wall can carry on
nominal tie-down; anything stronger needs its own hold-down `[C]`.

Proprietary sheet bracing (fibre cement, hardboard, plasterboard systems, OSB) uses the
manufacturer's tested capacities - fetch the current manual and pass the kN/m figure.

Rules that go with the capacities:
- Nominal bracing (ordinary wall linings): 0.45 kN/m sheeted one side, 0.75 kN/m sheeted both
  sides; counts for no more than 50% of the racking force, only if evenly distributed, and
  only in lengths of 450 mm or more (Cl 8.3.6.2) `[C]`.
- Minimum sheet-bracing panel length 900 mm for the tabulated capacity `[C]`; shorter panels
  only at the reduced capacity the standard or manufacturer gives.
- Walls higher than 2700 mm: capacity reduced in inverse proportion to height (Cl 8.3.6.4,
  Table 8.19) `[C]`.
- Maximum spacing between bracing walls at right angles to the wind: 9000 mm for N1 and N2
  `[C]`; for N3 and N4 it depends on ceiling diaphragm depth and roof pitch (Cl 8.3.6.7) `[C]`.
- Spread bracing evenly; put it in external walls and at corners where possible.

`python scripts/as_calc.py as1684-bracing` adds up capacity against racking force for one
direction, applies the height factor and the 50% nominal cap, and sizes any shortfall.

## 6. Tie-down - Section 9

1. Tables 9.2 and 9.5 decide whether nominal fixings are enough or specific tie-down is
   needed. Specific tie-down is required for sheet roofs in N1 to N4 and tile roofs in N3
   and N4 `[C]`. Roof battens always need specific tie-down `[C]`.
2. Uplift force at a connection = net uplift pressure (Table 9.5 `[C]`, by connection
   position and roof type) x ULW x fixing spacing. The N1-N4 pressures are not reproduced
   here - read them from the table.
3. Choose a fixing from Tables 9.16-9.25 with at least that capacity for the timber's joint
   group (JD4, JD5 etc - `timber.md`).
4. Follow the load path through every joint to the ground: batten, rafter or truss, top
   plate, stud, bottom plate, floor frame or slab, footing. Lintels collect the uplift of
   every rafter over the opening and pass it to the jamb studs.

`python scripts/as_calc.py as1684-tiedown` does step 2 and compares with a connector capacity.

## 7. Notches and holes

Limits are in the member sections (bearers and joists in Section 4; studs and plates in
Cl 6.2.1.4 `[C]`). Typical figures, none of them confirmed - confirm the
exact rule for the member before noting it on a drawing:
- Studs: notches up to 20 mm deep for let-in bracing; holes up to 25 mm in the middle of the
  face; deeper notching needs a bigger or doubled stud.
- Joists and bearers: no notches or holes in the middle third of the span near the tension
  edge; end notches limited to a fraction of depth (D/4 typical).
- Top plates: where notched or cut for services more than the limit, strap or reinforce.
